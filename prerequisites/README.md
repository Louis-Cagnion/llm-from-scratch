# Prerequisites plan

**Summary.** Everything needed before building a language model by hand, starting from zero: mathematics, programming, systems and research method. 49 modules in four tracks, followed in seven stages from the most fundamental to the least; each module is validated by its own evaluation, and a final evaluation covers them all ([evaluation rules](../README.md#evaluations)).

## How to follow this plan

- Modules are identified by track and number (`MA07`); each one lists the modules it depends on. A module can start once its dependencies are validated, so the mathematics and programming tracks progress in parallel.
- Each module starts by offering its evaluation directly; passing it validates the module without following it.
- Each module has its own file with objectives, the competences its evaluation tests, notions, practice programs and references; titles below link to the files already written.
- Recommended sequence, from the most fundamental to the least: seven stages, each one using only what the previous ones taught; inside a stage, modules are listed in their order of dependency.

1. **Foundations**: MA01, MA02, MA03, IN01, IN02, ME01.
2. **Core mathematics and programming**: MA04, MA05, MA06, MA07, MA08, IN03, IN04, IN05.
3. **Analysis, probability and algorithms**: MA09, MA10, MA11, MA12, MA13, IN06, IN07, IN08, SY01.
4. **Multivariable mathematics, statistics and systems**: MA14, MA15, MA16, MA17, MA18, IN09, IN10, SY02, ME02.
5. **Applied mathematics**: MA19, MA20, MA21, MA22, MA23, MA24, ME03.
6. **Advanced computer science and GPU**: MA25, MA26, IN11, IN12, IN13, SY03, SY04.
7. **Cryptography, security and cloud**: IN14, IN15, SY05.

## Track MA: mathematics

- **[MA01. Numbers and arithmetic](MA01-numbers-and-arithmetic.md)** (depends on: none): natural numbers and integers, operations and precedence, divisibility, Euclidean division, GCD and LCM, prime numbers, fractions, decimals, percentages, integer powers, square roots, scientific notation, orders of magnitude, rounding.
- **[MA02. Elementary algebra](MA02-elementary-algebra.md)** (MA01): algebraic expressions, expanding and factoring, notable identities, linear equations and inequalities, quadratic equations (discriminant), systems of linear equations (2×2, 3×3), absolute value, rational exponents.
- **[MA03. Logic, sets and proofs](MA03-logic-sets-and-proofs.md)** (MA02): propositions, connectives, quantifiers, implication and equivalence, negation, sets and set operations, Cartesian product, ∑ and ∏ notation, functions between sets (injective, surjective, bijective), order and equivalence relations, proof techniques (direct, contrapositive, contradiction, induction, counterexample).
- **[MA04. Real functions](MA04-real-functions.md)** (MA02, MA03): domain, range, graph, linear and affine functions, polynomials, rational functions, square root, absolute value, floor; composition, inverse function, monotonicity, parity, periodicity, graph transformations, piecewise functions.
- **[MA05. Exponential and logarithm](MA05-exponential-and-logarithm.md)** (MA04): exp, ln, log₂, log₁₀, algebraic properties, real powers, comparative growth, logarithmic scales, sigmoid, tanh and softplus as functions.
- **[MA06. Trigonometry and complex numbers](MA06-trigonometry-and-complex-numbers.md)** (MA04, MA05): radians, unit circle, sin, cos, tan, addition and double-angle identities; complex numbers in algebraic, trigonometric and exponential form, Euler's formula, modulus and argument, multiplication as rotation, roots of unity.
- **[MA07. Sequences and series](MA07-sequences-and-series.md)** (MA03, MA05): arithmetic and geometric sequences, recurrences, limits and convergence, monotone bounded sequences, series, geometric series, convergence tests, power series, Taylor series of exp, ln, sin and cos (basis of the hand-written math library), exponential moving averages (basis of Adam).
- **[MA08. Linear algebra I](MA08-linear-algebra-i.md)** (MA02, MA03): vectors of ℝⁿ, linear combinations, dot product, norms (L1, L2, L∞), angles, cosine similarity, matrices, matrix product and its cost, transpose, special matrices (identity, diagonal, triangular, symmetric), linear systems and Gaussian elimination, inverse, determinant, rank.
- **[MA09. Limits and continuity](MA09-limits-and-continuity.md)** (MA07): limits of functions, indeterminate forms, ε-δ definition, continuity, intermediate value theorem, asymptotes, Landau notation (o, O).
- **[MA10. Differentiation](MA10-differentiation.md)** (MA06, MA09): difference quotient, derivative, tangent line, rules (sum, product, quotient, chain rule), derivatives of usual functions (including sigmoid, tanh, softplus and the tanh approximation of GELU), function analysis, extrema, convexity, mean value theorem, Taylor expansions, L'Hôpital's rule, Newton's method.
- **[MA11. Integration](MA11-integration.md)** (MA10): antiderivatives, Riemann integral and sums, fundamental theorem of calculus, integration by parts, substitution, improper integrals, Gaussian integral, error function erf, numerical integration (rectangle, trapezoid, Simpson).
- **[MA12. Discrete probability](MA12-discrete-probability.md)** (MA01, MA03, MA07): random experiments, sample space, events, axioms, counting (arrangements, combinations, binomial theorem), conditional probability, Bayes' rule, independence, discrete random variables, distribution, expectation, variance, linearity, Bernoulli, binomial, geometric, Poisson, categorical and multinomial distributions.
- **[MA13. Discrete mathematics and graphs](MA13-discrete-mathematics-and-graphs.md)** (MA03, MA12): recurrences, structural induction, directed and undirected graphs, paths, connectivity, directed acyclic graphs, topological sort (basis of automatic differentiation), trees, heaps, asymptotic complexity, advanced counting, modular arithmetic basics, hashing and Jaccard similarity (basis of MinHash).
- **[MA14. Linear algebra II](MA14-linear-algebra-ii.md)** (MA08): vector spaces, subspaces, linear independence, bases, dimension, linear maps, kernel and image, rank theorem, change of basis, eigenvalues and eigenvectors, diagonalization, spectral theorem, orthogonal matrices, orthogonal projections, Gram-Schmidt, QR decomposition, singular value decomposition, low-rank approximation (Eckart-Young, basis of LoRA), matrix norms (Frobenius, spectral), condition number, positive definite matrices, Kronecker product, tensors of any order, Einstein summation (einsum).
- **[MA15. Multivariable calculus](MA15-multivariable-calculus.md)** (MA10, MA11, MA08): functions from ℝⁿ to ℝᵐ, partial derivatives, gradient, directional derivative, differential, Jacobian, multivariable chain rule, Hessian, second-order Taylor expansion, unconstrained extrema, multivariable convexity, Lagrange multipliers, multiple integrals, change of variables (Jacobian determinant).
- **[MA16. Matrix calculus](MA16-matrix-calculus.md)** (MA14, MA15): differentials of matrix functions, gradients with respect to vectors and matrices, layout conventions, trace and product identities, derivatives of the matrix product, softmax (Jacobian), cross-entropy, LayerNorm, RMSNorm and attention, checked by finite differences.
- **[MA17. Continuous probability and limit theorems](MA17-continuous-probability-and-limit-theorems.md)** (MA11, MA15, MA12): density and cumulative distribution functions, uniform, exponential and normal distributions (Φ and the exact GELU with its derivative), joint, marginal and conditional distributions, covariance, correlation, Gaussian vectors, change of variables, law of large numbers, central limit theorem, Markov, Chebyshev and Hoeffding inequalities, sampling (inverse transform, Box-Muller, rejection), Markov chains (transition matrix, stationary distribution), hidden Markov models, Monte Carlo methods, importance sampling.
- **[MA18. Statistics](MA18-statistics.md)** (MA17): samples, estimators, bias, variance, mean squared error, maximum likelihood (link with cross-entropy), Bayesian estimation and MAP (link with regularization), Fisher information, latent-variable models and the EM algorithm (Gaussian mixtures, k-means as its hard version), variational inference and the evidence lower bound (basis of variational autoencoders), confidence intervals, hypothesis tests (sign test, paired tests, chi-square and goodness-of-fit), p-values, multiple comparisons, bootstrap, least-squares linear regression, overfitting and the bias-variance trade-off.
- **MA19. Information theory** (MA05, MA12, MA17): information content, entropy, joint and conditional entropy, cross-entropy, Kullback-Leibler divergence (asymmetry, non-negativity), mutual information, perplexity, bits per byte, source coding (Huffman, arithmetic coding, entropy coders such as FSE), the link between prediction and compression, maximum entropy.
- **MA20. Optimization** (MA15, MA16): optimization problems, convexity, optimality conditions, gradient descent (step size, convergence on smooth convex functions), Newton's method, quasi-Newton methods (BFGS, L-BFGS), stochastic gradient descent, momentum, Nesterov, AdaGrad, RMSProp, Adam (bias correction), conditioning and non-convex landscapes (saddle points), constrained optimization (Lagrange, KKT, duality), L1 and L2 regularization, robust losses (Huber), line search.
- **MA21. Numerical analysis and floating point** (MA07, MA10, MA18): binary representation, IEEE 754 (float64, float32, float16, bfloat16, fp8 E4M3 and E5M2), microscaling formats (MXFP8, MXFP4, NVFP4), rounding, machine epsilon, ULP, overflow and underflow, cancellation, error accumulation (Kahan summation), numerical stability (log-sum-exp, stable softmax), conditioning of a computation, function evaluation (argument reduction, Taylor and minimax polynomials, Newton for square root and reciprocal), interpolation (linear, bilinear, bicubic), finite differences and step choice, pseudo-random generators (linear congruential, xorshift, PCG) and their statistical tests.
- **MA22. Signal processing** (MA06, MA07, MA11, MA08): periodic signals, Fourier series, discrete Fourier transform, fast Fourier transform, discrete cosine transform (JPEG), discrete convolution and correlation, filters, sampling theorem, aliasing and resampling filters, frequencies and wavelengths (rotary position embeddings and their extension).
- **MA23. Ordinary and stochastic differential equations** (MA10, MA11, MA14, MA17): first-order linear equations, linear systems, matrix exponential, stability, discretization (Euler, zero-order hold), continuous-time linear state-space models (basis of Mamba); random walks and Brownian motion, stochastic differential equations and the Euler-Maruyama scheme, Fokker-Planck equation (intuition), score function and probability-flow ODE (basis of diffusion models).
- **MA24. Numerical linear algebra** (MA14, MA21): LU and Cholesky factorizations, triangular solves, power iteration, QR algorithm and Jacobi method for eigenvalues, computing an SVD, Newton-Schulz iteration (orthogonalization, used by the Muon optimizer), Hadamard transform (rotation-based quantization), principal component analysis, randomized methods, numerical stability of these algorithms.
- **MA25. Reinforcement learning foundations** (MA17, MA18, MA20): multi-armed bandits and exploration, Markov decision processes, returns and discounting, value and action-value functions, Bellman equations, dynamic programming (value and policy iteration), Monte Carlo methods, temporal-difference learning (TD(0), TD(λ)), the policy gradient theorem, REINFORCE, baselines and advantages, actor-critic, generalized advantage estimation, off-policy learning and importance weights, KL-regularized objectives.
- **MA26. Number theory, finite fields and elliptic curves** (MA13, MA14): extended Euclidean algorithm, modular inverse, fast modular exponentiation, Fermat and Euler theorems, Chinese remainder theorem, primality tests, groups, rings and fields, prime fields GF(p), binary fields GF(2ⁿ) as polynomials (AES, GHASH), elliptic curves over finite fields (Montgomery form for X25519, Weierstrass form for P-256), the discrete logarithm problem, the mathematics of RSA.

## Track IN: programming

- **[IN01. Linux and the terminal](IN01-linux-and-the-terminal.md)** (none): file system, paths, permissions, bash shell (commands, redirections, pipes, scripts, environment variables), processes and signals, a text editor, package management, SSH.
- **[IN02. Python I](IN02-python-i.md)** (IN01): interpreter, types (int, float, str, bool, None), variables, operators, conditions, loops, functions, scope, strings and formatting, lists, tuples, dictionaries, sets, comprehensions, files, exceptions, standard library modules.
- **[IN03. Git](IN03-git.md)** (IN01): repository, commits, staging area, history, branches, merging, conflicts, rebasing, remotes, hooks, commit messages, `.gitignore` (data and weights kept out of the repository).
- **[IN04. Python II](IN04-python-ii.md)** (IN02): object-oriented programming (classes, inheritance, composition, special methods and operator overloading, the basis of a `Tensor` class), iterators and generators, decorators, closures, context managers, type hints, dataclasses, recursion, modules and packages, virtual environments.
- **[IN05. C I](IN05-c-i.md)** (IN01): compilation chain (preprocessor, compiler, linker, Makefile), types, operators, control flow, functions, pointers, arrays, strings, structures, unions, enumerations, dynamic allocation, input and output, headers, multi-file programs.
- **[IN06. Python III](IN06-python-iii.md)** (IN04): memory model (references, mutability, reference counting, garbage collection), bytes, bytearray, `struct`, `array`, memoryview, binary files and `mmap`, serialization formats (and why `pickle` is unsafe), concurrency (threads and the GIL, multiprocessing, asyncio, selectors), sockets, subprocess, argparse, logging, performance measurement (time, timeit, cProfile).
- **[IN07. Algorithms and data structures](IN07-algorithms-and-data-structures.md)** (IN04, MA12, MA13): complexity, dynamic arrays, linked lists, stacks, queues, hash tables, binary search trees, heaps and priority queues, tries, graphs (breadth-first and depth-first search, shortest paths), sorting (merge sort, quicksort, heapsort), binary search, divide and conquer, dynamic programming (edit distance, Viterbi, longest common subsequence), greedy algorithms (Huffman), backtracking.
- **[IN08. The client side of the web](IN08-the-client-side-of-the-web.md)** (IN02): HTML, CSS (flexbox, grid, responsive layout), SVG, JavaScript (types, functions, objects, DOM, events, promises and async, fetch, streams), accessibility.
- **[IN09. C II](IN09-c-ii.md)** (IN05, SY01): function pointers, allocation and alignment, memory layout of multidimensional arrays (row-major order, strides), bit manipulation, undefined behavior, sanitizers and Valgrind, gdb, shared libraries called from Python through `ctypes`, the CPython C API, POSIX threads, atomics and the memory model, SIMD intrinsics (SSE, AVX2, FMA), optimization (profiling, `-O2`, profile-guided optimization).
- **[IN10. Testing, debugging and code quality](IN10-testing-debugging-and-code-quality.md)** (IN04, IN05): unit tests (`unittest`), property-based tests, test-driven development, numerical regression tests (tolerances in ULP), gradient checking, reproducibility (seeds), debuggers (pdb, gdb), logging, code review, the quality criteria applied in this project.
- **IN11. Networking and the server side of the web** (IN06): TCP/IP model, TCP sockets, HTTP/1.1 (requests, responses, headers, status codes, chunked transfer, range requests, multipart/form-data), an HTTP server and an HTTP client written on raw sockets, Server-Sent Events, JSON-RPC, latency and bandwidth.
- **IN12. Formal languages and parsing** (IN07, MA13): alphabets and languages, regular expressions, NFA and DFA, Thompson and subset constructions, minimization, finite-state tokenizers (the HTML tokenizer state machine), context-free grammars, derivations and ambiguity, pushdown automata, recursive descent, LL(1), LR (shift-reduce) and Earley parsing, hand-written parsers for JSON, JSON Schema and Markdown (CommonMark block and inline structure), templates (a small Jinja-like template language).
- **IN13. Compilers** (IN12, IN09): front end and back end, abstract syntax trees, intermediate representations, static single assignment form, dataflow analysis, common subexpression and dead code elimination, loop transformations (tiling, fusion, unrolling), instruction scheduling, register allocation, code generation (emitting C and PTX text), just-in-time compilation.
- **IN14. Cryptography and TLS** (MA26, IN09, IN12): security goals, hash functions (SHA-1 and SHA-256 written by hand), HMAC, HKDF, password hashing (PBKDF2, memory-hard functions), cryptographically secure random numbers from operating system entropy, AES written by hand, modes of operation (CTR, GCM with GHASH), key exchange (Diffie-Hellman, X25519), signatures (RSA PKCS#1 v1.5 and PSS, ECDSA P-256) and their verification, base64, ASN.1 DER, X.509 certificates and chain validation, constant-time code, the TLS 1.3 protocol (handshake, key schedule, record layer, alerts).
- **IN15. Application security** (IN11, IN08, IN14, SY02): injections (command, path traversal), XSS and escaping, CSRF, browser isolation (same-origin policy, iframe sandbox, Content Security Policy), authentication, sessions, API keys and password storage, secrets and their redaction in logs, unsafe deserialization, sandboxing (process isolation, resource limits, Linux namespaces, seccomp), least privilege, supply chain.

## Track SY: systems and performance

- **[SY01. Computer architecture](SY01-computer-architecture.md)** (MA01, IN05): binary and hexadecimal, signed integers (two's complement), the processor (instruction set, pipeline, branch prediction, out-of-order execution), memory hierarchy (registers, L1 to L3 caches, cache lines, TLB, DRAM), bandwidth and latency, SIMD, multicore and cache coherence, the roofline model and arithmetic intensity, PCIe.
- **[SY02. Operating systems](SY02-operating-systems.md)** (IN05, SY01): processes and threads, scheduling, virtual memory (pages, huge pages, `mmap`), files and I/O, signals, inter-process communication (pipes, shared memory, Unix sockets), resource limits (cgroups), synchronization (mutexes, semaphores, deadlocks).
- **SY03. GPU architecture and CUDA** (IN09, SY01, SY02): installing the CUDA toolkit, execution model (grids, blocks, warps, threads, streaming multiprocessors), memory hierarchy (global, shared, registers, caches, constant), coalescing, bank conflicts and swizzled shared-memory layouts, occupancy, register pressure and `__launch_bounds__`, divergence, synchronization, parallel reductions and scans, streams and events, host-device transfers, unified memory, asynchronous copies (`cp.async`), `ldmatrix`, tensor cores (WMMA, `mma`), inline PTX, reading SASS (cuobjdump, nvdisasm), fp16 and bf16 on GPU, tools (nvcc, cuda-gdb, compute-sanitizer, Nsight), specifics of the RTX 3070 Laptop (Ampere, sm_86, 8 GB).
- **SY04. Parallel and distributed computing** (SY02, IN11): data and task parallelism, Amdahl's law, shared memory versus message passing, collective operations (broadcast, reduce, all-reduce, all-gather, reduce-scatter, all-to-all) and their algorithms (ring, tree), the latency-bandwidth cost model, interconnects (PCIe, NVLink and NVSwitch, InfiniBand and RoCE, RDMA, GPUDirect), CUDA inter-process communication and peer access, fault tolerance and checkpoints.
- **SY05. The cloud** (IN01, SY04): concepts (infrastructure as a service, virtual machines, GPU instances, object storage, networking, regions), providers and pricing models (on demand, interruptible, reserved), how the cost of a run is computed, SSH access, security (keys, firewall, secrets), cost control (budgets, alerts, automatic shutdown), data transfer. The decision to rent or not is taken later, in module L38, once training costs can be estimated.

## Track ME: method

- **[ME01. Technical English and reading papers](ME01-technical-english-and-reading-papers.md)** (none): machine learning vocabulary, structure of a research paper (abstract, method, experiments, appendices), active reading, reproducing an equation or a table, arXiv, following a bibliography.
- **[ME02. Machine learning concepts](ME02-machine-learning-concepts.md)** (MA18): supervised, unsupervised, self-supervised and reinforcement learning, training, validation and test data, generalization, overfitting, loss functions, metrics, a short history of artificial intelligence up to large language models.
- **ME03. Experimental method** (MA18, IN10, IN08): hypothesis, baseline, ablation, one change at a time, measurement noise, repetitions, statistical significance, experiment log, plotting results with hand-made SVG charts, reproducibility.

## Final evaluation

Taken once all 49 modules are validated: one session per track (mathematics in several sessions), testing every competence of every module at least once with exercises never seen before (neither in learning nor in a module evaluation): calculations and proofs, programs in Python, C and CUDA, and explanations. Each session requires 100 %; a failed session is retaken alone, with new exercises, after a review of the modules concerned. The final evaluation is passed when every session is.
