# MA04. Real functions

| Track | Stage | Depends on | Needed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | 2. Core mathematics and programming | MA02, MA03 | MA05, MA06 |

## Why this module

A neural network is a composition of functions; an activation, a loss, a learning-rate schedule and a probability distribution are all functions whose shape matters. Reading a graph, composing functions and inverting them are daily gestures in the LLM plan.

## Objectives

After this module, you can describe, sketch and transform the usual real functions, compose and invert them, and reason about their domain, monotonicity and symmetries.

## Competences evaluated

1. Determine the domain and the range of a function given by a formula.
2. Read information from a graph (values, preimages, sign, variations, extrema) and sketch the graph of a function from its formula.
3. Know the graphs and properties of the reference functions: affine, quadratic, cubic, power, inverse (1/x), square root, absolute value, floor.
4. Study polynomial and rational functions: roots, sign table, behavior for large |x|, vertical asymptotes.
5. Compose functions, give the domain of a composition, and decompose a function into simpler ones.
6. Decide whether a function is invertible on an interval and compute its inverse; relate the graphs of a function and its inverse.
7. Determine monotonicity on intervals without calculus (from definitions and composition rules).
8. Recognize even, odd and periodic functions and use these properties.
9. Apply graph transformations (shifts, stretches, reflections) and give the formula of a transformed function.
10. Define and use piecewise functions (including ReLU-like functions) and solve equations and inequalities graphically.

## Notions, in learning order

1. **Functions**: definition, formula, table, graph, domain, codomain, range, preimage.
2. **Reference functions**: affine (slope, intercept), quadratic (vertex form), cubic, powers, 1/x, √x, |x|, floor.
3. **Polynomials and rational functions**: degree, roots and factorization, sign tables, polynomial division, asymptotes.
4. **Operations on functions**: sum, product, quotient, composition, decomposition.
5. **Inverse functions**: bijection on an interval, computing the inverse, symmetry of graphs about y = x.
6. **Monotonicity**: increasing and decreasing functions, monotonicity of compositions.
7. **Symmetries**: even and odd functions, periodicity.
8. **Transformations**: f(x + a), f(x) + b, a·f(x), f(a·x), reflections.
9. **Piecewise functions**: definition, graphs, ReLU and its relatives, solving equations graphically.

## Practice

- Sketching by hand dozens of graphs, then checking them.
- Recognizing a function from its graph and writing its formula.
- Once IN02 is validated: a small program that evaluates a function on a grid and draws its graph as text in the terminal (an ASCII plotter), reused for all later function modules.

## Evaluation format

One written session, about 1 hour 30: around 15 exercises covering every competence, including sketches by hand. Pass mark 100 %.
