# MA02. Elementary algebra

| Track | Stage | Depends on | Needed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | 1. Foundations | MA01 | MA03, MA04, MA08 |

## Why this module

Algebra is the language every later formula is written in: a loss function, a learning-rate schedule, the variance of an initialization, the cost of attention are all algebraic expressions to expand, factor, rearrange and solve. Without fluent algebra, every derivation of the LLM plan becomes a wall.

## Objectives

After this module, you can manipulate any algebraic expression without error, solve linear and quadratic equations and inequalities, solve small linear systems, and translate a word problem into equations.

## Competences evaluated

1. Translate a sentence into an algebraic expression or equation, and back.
2. Evaluate, expand, factor and simplify algebraic expressions, including with the notable identities (a + b)², (a − b)², (a + b)(a − b).
3. Simplify algebraic fractions (rational expressions) and state the values where they are undefined.
4. Solve linear equations, including equations with fractions and parameters, and isolate any variable in a formula.
5. Solve linear inequalities, handle the sign change when multiplying by a negative number, and write solution sets as intervals.
6. Solve quadratic equations by factoring, completing the square and the quadratic formula; use the discriminant to count real solutions.
7. Solve equations and inequalities with absolute values.
8. Solve systems of two and three linear equations by substitution and by elimination; recognize systems with no solution or infinitely many.
9. Apply rational exponents and nth roots, and rationalize a denominator.
10. Model a word problem with equations, solve it and check that the answer makes sense.

## Notions, in learning order

1. **Variables and expressions**: letters as numbers, terms, coefficients, like terms, evaluating an expression.
2. **Expanding and factoring**: distributivity, common factors, the notable identities, factoring by grouping.
3. **Algebraic fractions**: simplification, operations, common denominators, forbidden values.
4. **Linear equations**: equivalent transformations, equations with fractions, equations with a parameter, rearranging formulas.
5. **Linear inequalities**: order and operations, sign change, intervals and their notation, compound inequalities.
6. **Quadratic equations**: factoring, completing the square (and why it gives the quadratic formula), the discriminant, sum and product of the roots.
7. **Absolute value**: definition as a distance, equations and inequalities |x − a| ≤ r.
8. **Systems of linear equations**: substitution, elimination, geometric meaning (intersecting lines), consistent and inconsistent systems, three equations with three unknowns (a first look at Gaussian elimination).
9. **Rational exponents and roots**: a^(p/q), nth roots, rules, rationalizing denominators.
10. **Modeling**: from a text to equations, checking units and plausibility.

## Practice

- Paper drills on each notion, with self-checking by substituting the solution back.
- Rearranging real formulas of the field (for example the parameter count of a layer, or a compound learning-rate decay) to isolate a chosen quantity.
- Once IN02 is validated: a program that solves any quadratic equation (with the discriminant) and a program that solves a 2 × 2 or 3 × 3 system by elimination, with exact fractions.

## Evaluation format

One written session without a calculator, about 90 minutes: around 20 exercises covering every competence and 2 modeling problems. Pass mark 100 %.
